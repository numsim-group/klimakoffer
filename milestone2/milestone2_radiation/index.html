Introduction

The energy balance of Earth is strongly impacted by radiation. The following figure shows the energy fluxes in the global Earth atmosphere system.

  • Earth's Energy Budget. Source: NASA, Public domain, quoting Loeb et al., J. Clim 2009 & Trenberth et al, BAMS 2009.

Remark: Note that this figure is from 2010. By now the net absorbed energy has risen to 1.0 W/m21.0\; W/m^21.0W/m2. (Loeb et al. (2021). Satellite and Ocean Data Reveal Marked Increase in Earth’s Heating Rate. Geophysical Research Letters, 48(13))

In our EBM, we aim to include four effects:

(i) Incoming solar radiation.

(ii) Reflection of radiation by the surface and cloud cover.

(iii) Cooling because of outgoing longwave radiation.

(iv) Effect of greenhouse gases (mainly CO2CO_2CO2​).

Outgoing longwave radiation

The goal in this section is to define a model/parametrization for the source term SOLW(T,x,t)S_{OLW}(T,x,t)SOLW​(T,x,t).

But first, to warm up with the topic, we consider as a toy model an idealized black-body Earth, i.e., we assume that the Earth is a black body that emits and absorbs radiation in the infrared spectrum.

The radiation energy per time that is emitted by a black body can be computed by the Stefan-Boltzmann law of physics

I=σSB TR4, I = \sigma_{SB}\,T_R^4, I=σSB​TR4​,

where III is the radiation energy per time per area with units [W/m2][W/m^2][W/m2], TRT_RTR​ is the radiation temperature with physical units Kelvin [K][K][K], σSB=0.56687⋅10−8\sigma_{SB} = 0.56687\cdot 10^{-8}σSB​=0.56687⋅10−8 is the Stefan-Boltzmann constant with units [W/m2/K4][W/m^2/K^4][W/m2/K4].

This model is indeed as simple as it gets and it is no surprise that the quality of the prediction of an average Earth temperature is quite off. The black-body radiation temperature of Earth would be only about −18-18−18 degree Celsius, hence, some major modeling improvements are necessary.

Budyko's Empirical infrared Model

Budyko (1968) suggested an empirical linear model for the outgoing longwave radiation

I∼A+B T. I \sim A + B\, T. I∼A+BT.

It is in general motivated by available observational data, shown in the next figure

If we consider the temperature in units Kelvin, we can fit the observed data with the linear model by choosing good constants AAA and BBB to get

IIR/OLW=A+B (T−273.15), I_{IR/OLW} = A + B\,(T - 273.15), IIR/OLW​=A+B(T−273.15),

with A=210.3A=210.3A=210.3 as the radiative cooling in units [W/m2][W/m^2][W/m2], and B=2.15B=2.15B=2.15 the radiative cooling feedback with units [W/m2/K][W/m^2/K][W/m2/K]. It is important to note that the choice of these parameters has a direct impact on the outgoing radiation and hence on the cooling. Several others have fitted the data differently, hence some range of choices for AAA and BBB is available. The values we select are from the paper by Zhuang et al. (2017).

We are now able to consider a second, but hopefully improved toy EBM. We replace the crude black-body radiation with a phenomenological approximation of the outgoing radiation (the Budyko model) to get

(A+B (TR−273.15))4 π RE2=S0 (1−α) π RE2, (A + B\,(T_R - 273.15)) 4\,\pi\,R_E^2 = S_0\,(1-\alpha)\,\pi\,R_E^2, (A+B(TR​−273.15))4πRE2​=S0​(1−α)πRE2​,

which can be directly solved again to get the equilibrium temperature

TR=S04(1−α)−AB+273.15≈13 [∘C]. T_R = \frac{\frac{S_0}{4}(1 - \alpha) - A}{B} + 273.15 \approx 13\,[^\circ C]. TR​=B4S0​​(1−α)−A​+273.15≈13[∘C].

Remark 9: The phenomenological model of Budyko drastically improves the temperature prediction of Earth from the crude black-body temperature of −18-18−18 to the temperature 13∘C13^\circ C13∘C, compared to the observed value of about 14.5∘C14.5^\circ C14.5∘C.

Effect of greenhouse gases (CO2CO_2CO2​)

For the interested reader, we refer to chapter 4 of the book by Kim and North, "Energy Balance Climate Model", (2017, Wiley) and the following paper

Myhre, G., Highwood, E. J., Shine, K. P., & Stordal, F. (1998). New estimates of radiative forcing due to well mixed greenhouse gases. Geophysical research letters, 25(14), 2715-2718.

The Earth's atmosphere is composed of several gases, with the most abundant ones being nitrogen (N2N_2N2​), oxygen (O2O_2O2​), and argon (ArArAr). These gases do not strongly absorb infrared radiation, which is important because the Earth's surface emits infrared radiation as it cools down after being heated by the sun. N2N_2N2​ and O2O_2O2​ are diatomic molecules, meaning they consist of two atoms chemically bonded together. Diatomic molecules have no permanent dipole, which means they have no separation of electric charge and therefore do not strongly interact with infrared radiation. Argon, on the other hand, is a monatomic gas, meaning it consists of individual atoms rather than molecules. However, it does not absorb infrared radiation because it does not have any modes of rotation or vibration in the infrared spectrum.

Overall, the lack of strong absorption of infrared radiation by these main constituents of the atmosphere allows heat to escape from the Earth's surface and be radiated out to space, helping to regulate the planet's temperature.

H2OH_2OH2​O molecules, on the other hand, have a permanent dipole moment because the distribution of electrons in the molecule is asymmetric. As a result, H2OH_2OH2​O molecules respond strongly to passing electromagnetic waves, including those in the infrared spectrum, which leads to the absorption of infrared radiation.

Molecules such as CO2CO_2CO2​, CH4CH_4CH4​, and NO2NO_2NO2​ do not have a permanent dipole moment because they are symmetrical in shape. However, they can still absorb infrared radiation through the phenomenon of induced dipole moments. When an infrared photon passes near one of these molecules, it can cause the electrons in the molecule to shift slightly, resulting in a temporary dipole moment. This temporary dipole moment can then interact with the passing electromagnetic wave, leading to the absorption of infrared radiation.

The ability of these molecules to absorb infrared radiation is significant because it allows them to contribute to the greenhouse effect. The greenhouse effect is the process by which certain gases in the Earth's atmosphere, including CO2CO_2CO2​, CH4CH_4CH4​, and NO2NO_2NO2​, trap heat and warm the planet's surface. Without this natural process, the Earth's average temperature would be much lower and life as we know it would not be possible. However, when these gases are present in excess, they can cause an imbalance in the greenhouse effect, leading to climate change.

Comment: The effect of greenhouse gases is very complex and there is a lot of ongoing research. H2OH_2OH2​O in particular is very important but ever so complex due to it being dependent on many other components and processes, i.e., the full water cycle. We will consider in our EBM only the effect of CO2CO_2CO2​ on the radiation. Infrared absorbtion means that the efficiency of our outgoing radiation (cooling effect) decreases (which causes a higher equilibrium temperature). This decrease in efficiency is topic of many investigations and studies, e.g., by the IPCC.

SOLW(T,x,t,CO2)=−(A+B (T−273.15)−ΔA(CO2)), S_{OLW}(T,x,t,CO_2) = -(A + B\, (T - 273.15) - \Delta A(CO_2)), SOLW​(T,x,t,CO2​)=−(A+B(T−273.15)−ΔA(CO2​)),
